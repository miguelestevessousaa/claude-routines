AVIATION DAILY

Friday, July 31, 2026

Today's edition centers on inspection and durability — Pratt & Whitney's acquisition of Aiir Innovations brings AI-assisted borescope analysis to the GTF and F135 fleets just as its long-promised hot-section upgrade kit edges toward a year-end EASA sign-off. Two fresh Airworthiness Directives, one for CFM56-family HPT blades operating in South Asia and one for Rolls-Royce's Trent XWB fuel-hose assembly, land the same week Duncan Aviation, Lufthansa Technik and GE Aerospace's Turkish Technic tie-up all expand shop capacity, from business jets to widebodies. Underneath it all is a familiar refrain: a durability fix only helps an airline once there's a shop slot, a trained inspector and the right parts waiting on the other end.

01
FLAGGED FOR YOU

Pratt & Whitney acquires Aiir Innovations, rolls out AI-assisted borescope inspection

RTX
RTX / Pratt & Whitney · July 15, 2026 · read →

Pratt & Whitney has acquired Amsterdam-based Aiir Innovations and folded its AI software into its borescope inspection workflow, using it to analyze inspection video and flag defects for closer review instead of relying solely on manual review. The tool is already deployed on the V2500, with pilot programs completed on the GTF and F135 and portfolio-wide expansion planned; SVP Rob Griffiths says the goal is to "detect issues earlier, improve turnaround times, increase time on wing and reduce operational disruption."

P&W's GTF hot-section upgrade kit on track for year-end EASA approval

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · July 2026 · read →

The roughly 35-part hot-section kit carries advanced components from the newly certified PW1100G Advantage into in-service PW1100G engines, and Commercial Engines President Rick Deurloo expects it to "get over the finish line sometime by the end of this year, early next year" before being incorporated straight into MRO visits. Early allocation will favor operators in harsh desert and poor-air-quality environments, ahead of a wider fleet rollout aimed at roughly doubling on-wing life.

P&W's GTF recovery now hinges on shop capacity, not the engineering fix

Airways Mag
Airways Magazine / Leeham News · July 2026 · read →

GTF MRO capacity is up 300% since 2019 and 26% this year alone, Deurloo told reporters, with an additive-manufacturing repair process cutting turnaround time by more than 60% — but one A320neo operator still expects the powder-metal fallout to linger for another two or three years. As Leeham puts it, "a durability fix does not help an airline unless an engine can get a shop slot, receive the right parts, complete the required work, and return to wing," with all affected A220s targeted to be flying again by year-end.

Duncan Aviation opens expanded PW300/PW500 overhaul shop in Lincoln

Aviation Week
Aviation Week / Duncan Aviation · July 2026 · read →

Duncan Aviation dedicated a 36,000-sq-ft expansion of its Lincoln, Nebraska turbine engine overhaul shop, adding twelve maintenance bays, expanded backshops and a new Atec-built test cell as a Pratt & Whitney Canada Designated Overhaul Facility for the PW300 and PW500. The PW300 covers Falcon 2000-series and 7X business jets and the Learjet 60, while the PW500 serves the Phenom 300 and Citation Bravo — a bet on faster turnaround and new international business-aviation engine work.

FAA orders repetitive borescope inspections on CFM HPT blades after South Asia distress reports

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 27, 2026 · read →

The new directive covers CFM International engines after updated service data showed engines operating in the South Asia region are susceptible to accelerated HPT stage-1 rotor blade deterioration and airfoil distress. It mandates initial and repetitive borescope inspections of those blades, with reduced inspection intervals or outright blade replacement required depending on what the BSI turns up.

06
FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA mandates fuel-hose inspections on Rolls-Royce Trent XWB after in-flight engine fire

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 17, 2026 · read →

The directive adopts EASA's mandate on Trent XWB-75/79/79B/84/97 engines after damage to a main fuel hose assembly caused a controlled, temporary engine fire, heat damage to the nacelle thrust-reverser C-ducts, and a commanded in-flight shutdown. It requires a one-time visual and dimensional inspection of the fuel manifold's main hose for softness, shrinkage, cracks, chafing, kinks and braid-wire degradation, with replacement where the hose fails those checks.

07

Rolls-Royce says Trent 1000 XE durability fix has "effectively eliminated" AOG

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

Nearly half of the Trent 1000 TEN fleet has now been retrofitted with the improved high-pressure-turbine blades that define the Trent 1000 XE standard, and Rolls-Royce says the upgrade has essentially eliminated 787 aircraft-on-ground events through the first half of the year. Three airlines have already selected the XE and it's reportedly a live contender in several ongoing sales campaigns, as the engine claws back to durability parity with GE's rival GEnx.

08

GE Aerospace signs five-year electronics MRO deal with Turkish Technic

Aviation Pros
Aviation Pros · July 21, 2026 · read →

Signed at Farnborough, the five-year agreement authorizes Turkish Technic to perform GE Aerospace-licensed MRO on Data Link Control and Display Units out of its Istanbul facilities, backed by dedicated test equipment, OEM parts and technical data from GE. It's another sign OEMs are leaning on licensed regional partners rather than only in-house capacity to keep pace with electronics and avionics repair demand.

09

Lufthansa Technik and Cebu Pacific extend CFM56-5B engine MRO deal by 18 shop events

Aviation Pros
Aviation Pros · July 21, 2026 · read →

The three-year extension covers 18 additional CFM56-5B shop visits for Cebu Pacific's A320ceo fleet, performed at Lufthansa Technik's Hamburg and Dublin engine shops, with the first unit due into Dublin by month's end. Cebu Pacific frames it as closing out its legacy narrowbody-engine MRO tender as the carrier shifts its growth to the A320neo family.

10

Boeing completes 737 MAX 10 certification flight testing, eyes 2027 entry into service

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 28, 2026 · read →

The MAX 10 program flew 976 certification flights totaling more than 2,060 flight hours and roughly 1,040 ground-test hours across three aircraft, wrapping with a cabin-systems check of the lavatory smoke detector and speaker system. Both the MAX 10 and MAX 7 now await final FAA sign-off, with Boeing holding to a 2027 entry-into-service target for the largest member of the family.
