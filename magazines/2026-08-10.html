AVIATION DAILY

Monday, August 10, 2026
Pratt & Whitney is leaning hard into its recovery story today — AOGs down nearly 40% from last year's peak, a fresh Airworthiness Directive targeting pressure-burner sensors, and OGMA's Alverca line adding PW1900G capability to its GTF workload. The FAA, meanwhile, is turning its attention to GE's GEnx fleet with a fuel-hose Airworthiness Directive taking effect later this month. And across the industry, LEAP and GTF engines are entering their first big shop-visit wave together, with parts lead times stretching to 20–40 weeks and squeezing MRO capacity everywhere.

FAA orders fuel-hose replacement on GE GEnx-1B/-2B engines

federalregister.gov
Federal Register / FAA · Effective Aug 21, 2026 · read →

A new Airworthiness Directive covers the GEnx-1B and -2B family (787 and 747-8) after a defective fuel-tube hose connecting the main fuel-oil heat exchanger was traced to a manufacturing lot defect that caused cracking and fuel leaks. Affected hoses must be removed from service and replaced.
