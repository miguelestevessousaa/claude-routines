AVIATION DAILY

Monday, August 3, 2026

Three fresh Airworthiness Directives anchor this edition — a tightened CFM56-7B fan-blade inspection rushed out after this month's Ryanair/Malta Air uncontained failure, a GEnx fuel-hose replacement mandate, and a Rolls-Royce fix good enough that the FAA wants to remove an inspection requirement rather than add one. Around them, the GTF story keeps splitting in two: Pratt & Whitney banked over 800 GTF orders and commitments at Farnborough while Eagle Services Asia leans on robotics to squeeze 40% more throughput out of its Singapore shops just to keep pace. Layer on Rolls-Royce's second durability tranche for the Trent 1000, CFM's record LEAP-1A haul, and a new Singapore joint venture built solely to add LEAP shop capacity, and the throughline is unmistakable: every OEM is racing to make engines last longer because nobody can build shop capacity fast enough to cover for engines that don't.

01
FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA rushes tightened CFM56-7B fan-blade AD after Ryanair engine failure

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · proposed rule, July 31, 2026 · read →

The proposed AD would supersede 2018-26-01 on all CFM56-7B engines, adopting CFM's updated ultrasonic and eddy-current inspection procedures with an expanded inspection area for fan-blade dovetail cracking. The FAA is giving only 30 days for comment instead of the usual 45, citing both the need to align with EASA and a July 10 uncontained engine failure on a Malta Air (Ryanair) 737-800 — though it says it has no evidence yet tying that event to the unsafe condition this AD addresses.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA orders fuel-hose replacement on GE GEnx-1B/2B engines over cracking defect

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective August 21, 2026 · read →

The AD follows a fuel imbalance on a 787-8 powered by GEnx-1B70/P2 engines, traced to a manufacturing defect in specific hose-preform lot numbers that caused the fuel tube hose connecting the main fuel-oil heat exchanger to crack and leak. It applies across the GEnx-1B and GEnx-2B family and requires removing every affected hose assembly and replacing it with an eligible part — no inspect-and-monitor option, straight to mandatory replacement.

Rolls-Royce fix is good enough that FAA proposes dropping a Trent 1000 inspection AD

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · proposed rule, July 23, 2026 · read →

AD 2023-12-16 required repeated inspection of the Trent 1000 high-pressure-turbine triple seal for excessive wear, with replacement of the seal and the intermediate-pressure-turbine disk if wear was found. Rolls-Royce has since developed a modification that eliminates the underlying wear mechanism entirely, so this proposal would strike qualifying engine serial numbers from the AD's applicability — a rare directive that subtracts maintenance burden rather than adding it. Comments are open through September 8.

Pratt & Whitney's Eagle Services Asia leans on robotics to lift GTF output 40%

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

Singapore-based Eagle Services Asia, whose workload is now dominated by GTF shop visits tied to the powder-metal recall, opened a second facility ("Eagle 2") in 2024 and has boosted throughput 40% over two years to absorb the demand. Newly inducted engines now pass through a robotic camera rig — a cobot that snaps 200-300 photos per engine and auto-generates condition reports — before technicians ever pick up a wrench, cataloguing components and overall health straight off induction.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

PW1100G shop demand in H1 2026 already outpaces all of last year

Air Cargo Week
Air Cargo Week / Aviation Maintenance Magazine · July 2026 · read →

PW1100G engine-stand utilization hit 95% in the first half of 2026, and EngineStands.com leasing volumes for that fleet already exceeded the whole of 2025 in six months — even as average project duration fell from 245 days in 2024 to 123 days. Legacy CFM56 and V2500 shops aren't off the hook either: with aging 737NGs and A320ceos still flying hard, a jet doing five or six cycles a day can burn through a full 450-cycle task interval in a single summer, pushing the bottleneck straight into autumn.

P&W's GTF tops 800 orders and commitments for 2026 after Farnborough haul

RTX
RTX / Pratt & Whitney · July 22, 2026 · read →

The tally was padded by a run of Farnborough wins: British Airways selected GTF engines for up to 63 A320neo-family aircraft, BOC Aviation ordered up to 220 engines for up to 110 A320neos, and Tigerair Taiwan signed an MOU covering 15 A321neos. It's a striking order book for an engine program still working through a multi-year powder-metal durability fix — proof airlines are betting the recovery, not the recall, is the lasting story.

07

Rolls-Royce's second Trent 1000 durability package lands through 2026

Rolls-Royce
Rolls-Royce · 2026 · read →

Phase 2 adds a further 30% time-on-wing improvement across the Trent 1000 and Trent 7000 fleets through better combustor-tile coatings, revised HPT nozzle-guide-vane cooling, lighter and better-coated HP turbine blades, and a combustor-to-turbine interface borrowed from the Trent XWB-84 EP. The certified HPT blade entered new-production engines this year and started reaching the in-service fleet from April, with the rest of the package rolling out through the second half of 2026 — stacked with Phase 1, Rolls-Royce says it triples time on wing versus the original Trent 1000 TEN.

08

CFM leaves Farnborough with a record 1,000-engine LEAP order and a durability certification

GE Aerospace
GE Aerospace · July 24, 2026 · read →

GE Aerospace and CFM International closed nearly 1,800 engine agreements at the show, headlined by an IndiGo MOU for more than 1,000 LEAP-1A engines to power 510 A320neo-family jets — the largest single order in LEAP history. CFM also secured certification for LEAP-1B high-pressure-turbine upgrades meant to double time on wing in hot-and-harsh environments, plus initial certification of a reverse-bleed system that cuts fuel-nozzle replacements, as the LEAP program passes 10,000 engines delivered and 100 million flight hours.

09

GEnx-1B becomes GE's fastest widebody engine ever to hit 50 million flight hours

GlobeNewswire
GE Aerospace · July 19, 2026 · read →

The 787-powering GEnx-1B crossed 50 million flight hours in just over 14 years — the fastest pace of any GE Aerospace widebody — while logging a 99.98% dispatch reliability rate across more than 50 operators. GE credits on-wing tools including its 360 Foam Wash and Blade Inspection Tool for keeping the fleet flying, alongside a $1 billion 2026 investment in U.S. manufacturing capacity, over $100 million of it earmarked specifically for GEnx production.

10

SIA Engineering and Safran launch $118M joint venture purely to add LEAP shop capacity

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal / Safran · June 2026 · read →

Safran will hold 51% and SIA Engineering 49% of a new Singapore venture built to fold SIAEC's existing quick-turn LEAP work into a full-fledged overhaul shop for LEAP-1A and LEAP-1B, backed by roughly $118 million in planned investment. It's a pure capacity play rather than a technology story — one more sign OEMs increasingly see regional joint ventures, not just in-house expansion, as the fastest way to add engine shop slots.
