AVIATION DAILY

Wednesday, July 22, 2026

Today's edition is dominated by Pratt & Whitney's slow climb out of the GTF powder-metal crisis — MRO expansion, a Hot Section Plus upgrade kit, and Airbus openly conceding it can't paper over the shortfall with more LEAP engines. Layer on a fresh batch of FAA Airworthiness Directives spanning Rolls-Royce, CFM, and P&W Canada, plus signs that engine shop-visit demand is about to outrun global MRO capacity, and the throughline is clear: the industry has more airplanes than it has serviceable engines for them.

Pratt & Whitney expands MRO network to wrap up GTF durability problems

Leeham News
Leeham News and Analysis · July 15, 2026 · read →

P&W has invested hundreds of millions of dollars building, expanding, and licensing MRO facilities to fix the contaminated powdered-metal issue that has grounded as many as 650 GTF-powered A320neo-family jets at once. One operator told Leeham the PW1100G issues are likely to keep constraining the fleet for another two to three years.

GTF Hot Section Plus upgrade kit on track for year-end certification

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · July 2026 · read →

The kit swaps roughly 35 hot-section parts from the newer PW1100G Advantage into legacy engines. Commercial Engines President Rick Deurloo says EASA sign-off should land "by the end of this year, early next year," with harsh-environment operators — desert bases, poor air quality — first in line once MRO visits begin incorporating it.

FAA orders repetitive inspections of P&W Canada turbine exhaust frames for cracking

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 14, 2026 · read →

The new Airworthiness Directive mandates repetitive visual inspections of the turbine exhaust frame on affected Pratt & Whitney Canada engines for cracks, with replacement required depending on findings. A textbook example of an inspection-driven AD rather than a fleet grounding.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

Rolls-Royce Trent XWB AD follows in-flight fuel hose fire damage

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 17, 2026 · read →

The AD covers Trent XWB-75/-79/-79B/-84/-97 engines after a report of a damaged main fuel hose assembly on the fuel manifold that caused a controlled, temporary engine fire and heat damage to the nacelle. It requires a one-time visual and dimensional inspection of the hose assembly, with replacement if defects are found.

The FAA's latest CFM International AD lands the same month HAECO and GE/Safran are pouring capital into LEAP aftermarket capacity — a reminder that even the newest-generation engines are generating a steady drumbeat of compliance actions as they rack up cycles.

Airbus concedes it can't buy its way out of the P&W GTF shortfall with more LEAP engines

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

CEO Guillaume Faury says Airbus is unlikely to secure enough additional CFM LEAP-1A engines to offset the PW1100G delivery gap, since LEAP already powers roughly 70% of A320neos and CFM's own line is maxed out. Airbus has trimmed its A320 production-rate target to 70–75 a month by end-2027 largely because of the P&W bottleneck.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

Engine MRO demand set to outrun global shop capacity by 17%

Aviation Pros
Aviation Pros / Bain & IBA analysis · July 2026 · read →

LEAP and GTF engines are entering their first large-scale shop-visit wave together, and industry forecasts now see MRO demand exceeding available capacity by more than 17% before the decade is out. Turnaround times are already running 35% longer than pre-pandemic for legacy engines and over 150% longer for new-generation types.

HAECO becomes an Elite repair facility for Woodward's LEAP engine components

GlobeNewswire
GlobeNewswire · July 21, 2026 · read →

HAECO is now authorized to deliver OEM-approved repairs across Woodward's full LEAP-1A/-1B/-1C fuel control, actuation, and air-management portfolio, cementing its role as an Asia-Pacific support hub. It's the latest in a run of Elite LRSF deals — AFI KLM E&M and Lufthansa Technik signed similar agreements this year — as Woodward races to add authorized capacity ahead of the LEAP shop-visit wave.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

Robotic borescopes and CMMS tracking are reshaping engine inspection

Oxmaint
Oxmaint · July 2026 · read →

Robotic borescope systems now navigate turbine stages, combustion chambers, and compressor sections to capture high-definition imagery of blade erosion, thermal-fatigue cracks, and coating degradation without engine removal. Every defect image is linked to an engine serial number and can auto-trigger a maintenance work order — a meaningful shift for shops drowning in the current shop-visit backlog.
