AVIATION DAILY

Friday, August 14, 2026

Durability is the word of the week: GE's LEAP-1B hot-section kit and Pratt's GTF Hot Section Plus both cleared major milestones at Farnborough, each promising roughly double the time on wing that's been driving the shop-visit crush. That crush is real and getting worse — IATA and independent stand-utilization data both point to GTF and LEAP capacity running at or past its limit through the decade. Meanwhile a fresh cluster of Airworthiness Directives on Rolls-Royce Trent 1000, GE GEnx, and CFM56-7B engines, plus OGMA's growing GTF-adjacent MRO footprint in Portugal, keep the inspection queue full even as the fixes start arriving.

Pratt & Whitney's GTF Hot Section Plus kit on track for 2027 rollout

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · Aug 2026 · read →

Pratt & Whitney says its Hot Section Plus retrofit — roughly 35 components borrowed from the newer PW1100G Advantage engine — remains on schedule for EASA sign-off around year-end 2026, with installs beginning in MRO visits shortly after. Commercial Engines president Rick Deurloo says the kit delivers 90-95% of the Advantage's durability benefit and will be prioritized first for operators flying in harsh desert and poor-air-quality environments. It's the clearest sign yet that a fix for the PW1100G-JM's chronic durability shortfall is close to shipping.

Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 hit with new AD over HPC anti-rotation block risk

Federal Register
Federal Register · effective Aug 13, 2026 · read →

The FAA has adopted a new AD for Trent 1000-A/AE/C/CE/D/E/G/H engines after Rolls-Royce determined a high-pressure compressor mini-disc anti-rotation block could release into the HPC stage 5/6 disc and cone rotor rear shaft during operation. That's a rotor-integrity finding, not a routine wear item, and it lands on a Trent fleet that's only just been getting AOG events under control after years of durability-driven shop visits.

03

GE Aerospace and CFM certify LEAP-1B durability kit doubling hot-section life

GE Aerospace News
GE Aerospace News · Jul 2026 · read →

At Farnborough, the FAA and EASA certified GE and CFM's LEAP-1B high-pressure turbine durability kit, aimed at doubling on-wing time in hot-and-harsh operating environments — the same category of fix Pratt is chasing for the GTF. GE also secured initial certification of a reverse-bleed system meant to cut on-wing fuel-nozzle replacements, directly trimming line-maintenance burden for LEAP-1B operators.

FAA orders removal of defective GEnx fuel tube hoses after leak report

Federal Register
Federal Register · effective Aug 21, 2026 · read →

A new AD for GE GEnx engines follows a reported fuel leak traced to a defective fuel tube hose connecting the main fuel-oil heat exchanger. Operators must pull all affected hoses from service and replace them with eligible parts before the directive takes effect August 21, 2026 — a compact, engine-serial-driven job but one that adds to an already-stacked AD queue this month.

FAA proposes tighter fan blade inspections for CFM56-7B fleet

Federal Register
Federal Register · comments close Aug 31, 2026 · read →

A proposed AD would require initial and repetitive ultrasonic or eddy-current inspections of certain CFM56-7B fan blades for crack indications, with replacement mandated where cracks are found. It's a variation on an inspection theme the fan-blade fleet has seen before, but the compliance window and applicability have been tightened; public comment closes August 31, 2026.

PW1100G stands hit 95% utilization as GTF shop visits keep climbing

Aviation Pros
Aviation Pros · Aug 2026 · read →

Engine-stand leasing data shows PW1100G stands running at roughly 95% utilization through the first half of 2026, with leasing volumes already exceeding all of 2025. The one bright spot: average project duration on a PW1100G stand has dropped from 245 days in 2024 to 123 days now, suggesting the GTF inspection and repair pipeline is finally getting faster even as demand keeps rising.

07

IATA presses OEMs for "urgent action" on engine MRO bottlenecks

IATA
IATA · Jun 2026 · read →

IATA's analysis, produced with Emerton, put 648 GTF-powered aircraft — 28% of the fleet — on the ground as of last year, with shop visits for LEAP alone forecast to grow from 600-800 a year to over 5,000 by 2040. Director General Willie Walsh is calling for OEMs to widen parts availability, open up independent MRO access, and lock in long-term spare-parts pricing before durability problems compound further into the decade.

OGMA completes first C-390 Millennium overhaul for Hungarian Air Force

AviTrader
AviTrader · Jun 24, 2026 · read →

OGMA, at its Alverca, Portugal facility, completed the first scheduled 24-month maintenance inspection on a Hungarian Air Force C-390 Millennium, strengthening Embraer's European military support network. It's a data point on OGMA's broader growth path — the business is tracking toward $350-400M in 2026 revenue on the back of wins like the Jazz Aviation E175 support deal and an extended SkyWest heavy-maintenance agreement.

09

FAA clears higher takeoff weight for Boeing 787-9 and 787-10

Boeing
Boeing · Mar 23, 2026 · read →

The FAA certified an increased maximum takeoff weight for the 787-9 (+10,000 lb, to 571,500 lb) and 787-10 (+14,000 lb, to 574,000 lb), giving operators the option of roughly 300-400+ additional nautical miles of range or several tonnes of extra payload. Every 787-9/-10 assembled since December 2025 is structurally capable of the higher weight, and airlines can activate it at delivery or later — a manufacturing-line change with real downstream implications for structural life tracking.

Jet2 confirms LEAP-1A across its entire 146-aircraft A321neo orderbook

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · Jul 2026 · read →

Jet2 has selected CFM's LEAP-1A for a further 54 A321neos, completing engine selection across its full 146-aircraft A321neo orderbook plus nine leased jets. Twenty-nine LEAP-1A-powered A321neos are already flying for the carrier, adding another single-source LEAP operator to a fleet base that will lean heavily on CFM's global MRO network for the next decade of shop visits.
