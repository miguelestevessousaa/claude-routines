AVIATION DAILY

Wednesday, July 29, 2026

Engine reliability is turning a corner across the board this week — GE Aerospace posted record shop-visit output, Airbus and Pratt & Whitney are promising an end to the A220's GTF crisis by year-end, and Rolls-Royce's durability fixes are winning back Trent 1000 orders it lost to GE. Regulators are moving too: the FAA wants to lighten a Trent 1000 inspection mandate now that a hardware fix exists, even as Boeing and GE lead a new industry push to speed up certification itself. Underneath the good news, the industry's next-engine bet is splitting in two — CFM chasing an open-fan leap for the 2030s while Pratt & Whitney doubles down on the geared turbofan it's still busy repairing.

GE Aerospace posts record shop-visit output as Q2 revenue jumps 21%

GE Aerospace
GE Aerospace · July 16, 2026 · read →

Second-quarter revenue rose 21% to $13.3 billion and adjusted EPS climbed 22%, with commercial engine deliveries up 31% and LEAP deliveries up 41% in the first half. CEO Larry Culp credited "record internal shop visit output" for the strength in Commercial Engines & Services, and the company raised full-year profit guidance across both its commercial and defense units.

FAA proposes to ease Trent 1000 inspection mandate now that Rolls-Royce has a fix

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · proposed July 23, 2026 · read →

The proposed rule would supersede a 2023 directive that required recurring inspections of the Trent 1000's high-pressure turbine triple seal for excessive wear. Rolls-Royce has since developed a hardware modification that removes the underlying wear risk, letting the FAA drop qualifying engine serial numbers from the AD entirely — comments are open through September 8.

03

CFM and its owners commit over $2 billion to widen the open LEAP MRO network

GE Aerospace
GE Aerospace / CFM International · July 18, 2026 · read →

With more than 10,000 LEAP engines delivered, GE Aerospace and Safran Aircraft Engines are each committing over $1 billion and €1 billion respectively over five years to expand third-party shop capacity. Three new Premier MRO shops — MTU Maintenance Fort Worth, Iberia Maintenance, and SSAMC in Chengdu — have joined the network in the past 18 months as CFM works to shorten shop-visit turnarounds.

04
FLAGGED FOR YOU

Engine makers split on Boeing's next narrowbody: open fan versus a "low-risk" GTF

AP / Spokesman-Review
The Spokesman-Review (AP) · July 24, 2026 · read →

CFM is pitching Boeing an open-fan design targeting 20% better fuel efficiency by the 2030s, while Pratt & Whitney is pushing its proven geared-turbofan architecture as the safer bet, arguing the industry needs stability rather than another clean-sheet risk before 2040. Pratt says its A220 corrosion fixes are nearly complete, a track record it's using to argue that GTF technology — not a new architecture — should power Boeing's next jet.

05
FLAGGED FOR YOU

Airbus and Pratt & Whitney signal the A220 engine crisis will be over by year-end

MRO Business Today
MRO Business Today · July 16, 2026 · read →

Only 2-3% of the global A220 fleet is now grounded for engine issues, down from roughly 17% a year ago, as Pratt & Whitney works through reduced life limits on PW1500G and PW1900G high-pressure compressor front hubs tied to corrosion found during overhaul. Airbus and Pratt credit the recovery to engineering fixes plus expanded repair capacity, and both expect no A220s grounded for GTF problems by the close of 2026.

06

Rolls-Royce's Trent 1000 XE durability fix starts winning orders back from GE

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

Better-than-expected results from the related Trent 7000 durability upgrade — in some cases more than tripling time-on-wing — have Rolls-Royce confident the same fix will restore the Trent 1000 XE to parity with GE's GEnx on the 787. LATAM Airlines' recent selection of the Trent 1000 XE for three incoming 787s, after previously defecting to GE over durability problems, is the clearest sign yet the strategy is working.

07

Rolls-Royce drops baseline Trent XWB-84 from sales, pushes the EP as standard

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

Rolls-Royce will no longer offer the baseline Trent XWB-84 in A350-900 sales campaigns, switching exclusively to the Enhanced Performance variant. The EP fleet has delivered average fuel-burn savings of around 1.8% in service, beating the 1% Rolls originally forecast.

08

Boeing wraps 737 Max 7 and Max 10 certification flight testing

Reuters
Reuters / U.S. News · July 20, 2026 · read →

Deputy FAA Administrator Chris Rocheleau says certification of both variants is "closer than ever before," with the smaller Max 7 potentially cleared as soon as July or August and the larger Max 10 targeted before year-end. Boeing still expects first deliveries of both variants in 2027 if the remaining regulatory steps go to plan.

09

Boeing and GE Aerospace form industry coalition to push FAA to speed up certification

The Air Current
The Air Current · July 27, 2026 · read →

The new PRISM coalition — Partnership for Regulatory Innovation, Safety, and Modernization — brings together Boeing, GE Aerospace and other major manufacturers to work with the FAA on modernizing a certification process still recovering from the delays that followed the industry's recent safety and quality crises. The push comes as a wave of new aircraft types, from eVTOLs to eventual clean-sheet narrowbodies, heads toward the same regulatory bottleneck.

10

Airbus approves €5 billion buyback as Boeing edges it at Farnborough

Leeham News
Leeham News and Analysis · July 28, 2026 · read →

Airbus's board approved a three-year, €5 billion share buyback alongside a target of €12-13 billion in adjusted operating profit by 2029, even as Boeing narrowly out-ordered it at Farnborough, 173 aircraft to 154. Some observers see echoes of Boeing's own 1990s shift toward shareholder returns, questioning whether the payout program will crowd out funding for the clean-sheet jet Airbus is still weighing for the 2030s.
