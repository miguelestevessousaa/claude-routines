AVIATION DAILY

Thursday, July 30, 2026

Today's edition is about capacity, not durability — Delta TechOps, MTU's EME Aero and OGMA are all racing to open new GTF shop floors just as IATA warns that more slots alone won't fix the aftermarket without freer independent-MRO access and faster repair approvals. A fresh Airbus directive on A320neo pneumatic-valve seals lands the same week GE's GEnx shortfall keeps squeezing Boeing's 787 ramp, a reminder that new-engine supply problems and legacy shop backlogs are now two sides of the same bottleneck. Underneath it all, CFM's open-fan demonstrator quietly cleared its first design review — proof that even while today's engines fight for shop slots, the industry is already racing toward what replaces them.

FAA finalizes AD for A320neo HPV butterfly-seal clips after in-service failures

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · July 29, 2026 · read →

The directive covers the current-production A319neo/A320neo/A321neo family after reports of high-pressure-valve butterfly-seal retention clips rupturing, letting the seal escape its groove and raising internal leakage that can trigger a false HPV-failed alert and touch the engine bleed-air and pneumatic systems. It supersedes and widens an earlier directive addressing the same failure mode, following EASA's original AD from April 2025.

02

GE's GEnx shortfall keeps squeezing Boeing's 787 delivery ramp

FlightGlobal
FlightGlobal · July 2026 · read →

Boeing's push toward 10 787s a month still hinges on GE Aerospace stabilizing GEnx-1B shipment rates, even after GE recently lifted output to steady the Dreamliner line. Demand isn't the issue — United ordered 300 GEnx engines earlier this year to become the type's largest prospective operator — but the delivery mismatch shows engine supply, not airframe assembly, is commercial aviation's tightest constraint. Rolls-Royce is meanwhile using Trent 1000 durability fixes to chip away at GE's share, winning its first new 787 engine selection in nearly three years.

03
FLAGGED FOR YOU

IATA study: GTF and LEAP shop visits will triple by 2040, and capacity alone won't fix it

IATA
IATA / Emerton Data · June 24, 2026 · read →

The joint IATA-Emerton study puts GTF-powered aircraft groundings at a peak of 648 jets — 28% of the fleet — in March 2025, and projects annual GTF shop visits climbing from roughly 1,000 today past 2,000 by 2040, with LEAP visits rising from 600-800 to over 5,000 over the same span. Director General Willie Walsh's line is blunt: "manufacturers are investing in additional capacity, but capacity alone will not be enough," and IATA is pushing faster repair-approval timelines, wider independent-MRO access, and guaranteed long-term spare-parts availability.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

Delta TechOps' new Atlanta GTF shop targets 240 engines this year, 450 at full tilt

Aviation Pros
Aviation Pros · 2026 · read →

Delta TechOps' new 155,000-sq-ft, 60-bay shop at Hartsfield-Jackson is dedicated to Pratt & Whitney PW1100G and PW1500G overhauls for Delta's A321neo and A220 fleets plus outside MRO customers, targeting 240 engines serviced this year — up from 150 last year — on the way to 400-plus by 2030. Under its capacity deal with Pratt & Whitney, throughput will grow more than 30% to as many as 450 engines annually, while the team works to cut turnaround from around 120 days into the 70s.

Southwest flags a CFM LEAP disk life-limit cap as an availability risk

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · July 2026 · read →

Even as CFM posts record LEAP output, Southwest has raised concerns over a life-limit cap tied to disk durability, with an improved part not expected until the second half of 2026. It's a reminder that new-generation engine maturity issues don't vanish once production ramps up — they resurface later as fleet-wide availability risk, echoing the GTF's own durability-to-capacity trajectory a few years earlier.

MTU's EME Aero joint venture scales toward 600 GTF shop visits a year

MTU Aero Engines
MTU Aero Engines · 2026 · read →

MTU's Polish joint venture EME Aero delivered its 1,000th overhauled engine in March and opened a second test cell in Jasionka as it ramps toward 450 annual shop visits and about 1,000 employees — MTU calls it the fastest capacity build-out in company history. Combined with MTU Maintenance Zhuhai, the network's overall GTF capacity is climbing toward 600 shop visits a year, with throughput up 26% in 2025 as the bottleneck that peaked during the powder-metal crisis slowly eases.

07

CFM's open-fan RISE demonstrator clears preliminary design review

CFM International
CFM International / Safran · July 18, 2026 · read →

Five years into the RISE program, CFM says roughly 500 test campaigns across Open Fan, compact-core and hybrid-electric architectures have culminated in the Open Fan demonstrator passing its preliminary design review, with early wind-tunnel results already beating aeroacoustic maturation targets. Blade and outlet-guide-vane hardware is now in impact, ingestion, fatigue, icing and vibration testing ahead of full-scale ground tests later this decade and a flight demonstration on an Airbus A380 testbed.

Aviation Week · 2026 · read →

Embraer-owned OGMA has opened a dedicated Pratt & Whitney GTF maintenance line in Portugal, a bet the 108-year-old MRO expects will triple revenue from its engine business once the shop reaches full capacity. It's another node in P&W's expanding third-party GTF repair network at a moment when shop-slot availability, not engineering fixes, is the industry's binding constraint.

FTAI Aviation posts record Aerospace Products quarter, expands module network to Indonesia and Egypt

FTAI Aviation
FTAI Aviation · July 29, 2026 · read →

FTAI's second quarter brought $953 million in total revenue and $291 million in adjusted EBITDA, led by a record $692 million from its module-swap Aerospace Products business alongside $183 million in engine maintenance and repair-exchange revenue. The company is pushing that module-exchange model — used heavily on CFM56 and V2500-era engines — into new maintenance hubs in Indonesia and Egypt as it chases faster, cheaper alternatives to a full shop visit.

Narrowbody engine shop visits have doubled since 2014, and supply still trails demand

IBA
IBA · 2026 · read →

IBA estimates narrowbody shop-visit inductions have grown from about 2,400 in 2014 to roughly 5,000 in 2025, powered first by CFM56 and V2500 volume and now increasingly by LEAP and GTF. LEAP turnarounds averaged 230 days in 2025 — an improvement on the PW1100G's 11-month peak during the powder-metal groundings, but still evidence of an industry short on slots, parts and labor even as GE Aerospace alone commits roughly $1 billion to expand capacity.
