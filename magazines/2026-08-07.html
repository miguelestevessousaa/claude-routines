AVIATION DAILY

Friday, August 7, 2026

The PW1100G recovery story keeps compounding: RTX's Q2 print shows fleet AOG counts down 25% year-to-date as MRO output climbs, while EASA's GTF Advantage certification and a fresh Hot Section Plus retrofit give operators two paths to more durable engines this year. Pratt & Whitney is backing that turnaround with capacity money in Georgia and Portugal, even as the FAA keeps grinding out fan-blade and fuel-line directives on the CFM56 and GEnx fleets that still fill most of the world's shops. Underneath it all, a quieter layer of MRO deal-making — HAECO into Woodward's LEAP network, SR Technics into Oman Air — shows how much of the aftermarket is being carved up before the next durability wave even lands.

PW1100G AOG count falls 25% YTD as RTX reports surging GTF demand

Investing News
Investing News Network · July 2026 · read →

RTX's second-quarter results show aircraft-on-ground counts for the PW1100G family down another 25% year-to-date, backed by a 40%-plus jump in MRO output and a 23% cut in turnaround times. Pratt & Whitney also logged more than 800 new GTF orders and commitments so far in 2026, pushing its backlog past 8,000 engines across 90-plus customers.

GTF Advantage clears EASA as Hot Section Plus retrofit lands this year

Asian Aviation
Asian Aviation · 2026 · read →

EASA certified the more durable PW1100G GTF Advantage for the A320neo family on April 17, promising roughly double the time-on-wing of the standard engine. A companion Hot Section Plus retrofit package brings much of that same durability gain to in-service baseline engines during normal shop visits later this year, without waiting on a full Advantage swap.

PW1100G stands hit 95% utilization as turnaround times halve

PW1100G engine stands reached 95% utilization industry-wide in early 2026, with average project duration dropping from 245 days to 123 days. Legacy CFM56-5A/B and -7B demand stayed elevated too, as operators lean on aging A320 and 737 fleets while new-aircraft deliveries lag — setting up a tight fight for shop capacity this autumn.

OGMA, the Embraer-majority-owned Portuguese MRO, opened a dedicated GTF engine shop that executives expect to triple the company's engine MRO revenue once it reaches full capacity. Portugal's defense minister joined Pratt & Whitney and Embraer leadership at the opening, underscoring how central GTF work has become to OGMA's growth since its 2020 P&W partnership.

Pratt & Whitney pours $70M into Columbus GTF overhaul capacity

Aero News Journal
Aero News Journal · 2026 · read →

Pratt & Whitney has opened an 81,000-square-foot GTF MRO expansion at its Columbus Engine Center in Georgia, the result of a $70 million investment aimed at chipping away at the industry's engine-overhaul backlog. The buildout is part of a broader 2026 plan to fold GTF Advantage hot-section technology into upgrade kits for in-service PW1100G engines.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA orders fuel-hose fix on GE GEnx engines after in-flight leak report

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective August 21, 2026 · read →

A new Airworthiness Directive on GE GEnx engines, effective August 21, was prompted by a reported fuel leak traced to a defective fuel tube hose connecting the main fuel-oil heat exchanger. Operators must remove all affected hoses from service and replace them with approved parts — a straightforward but fleet-wide parts swap for 787 and 747-8 operators.

07

FAA proposes renewed fan-blade inspections for the CFM56-7B fleet

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · comments due August 31, 2026 · read →

The FAA is proposing to supersede a 2018 directive on CFM56-7B engines — the workhorse powering most of the 737NG fleet — with updated initial and repetitive ultrasonic or eddy-current inspections of certain fan blades for crack indications. Blades that fail inspection must be replaced before further flight; the agency is taking public comment through August 31.

08

Pratt & Whitney Canada PW210 engines get crack-inspection mandate

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · June 2026 · read →

A new AD covers all P&WC PW210A, PW210A1 and PW210S engines after reports of circumferential cracking on the turbine exhaust frame's external surface, driven by thermal stress from repeated engine starts. The directive mandates repetitive visual inspections of the frame, with replacement required for any unit found cracked.

HAECO joins Woodward's elite repair network for CFM LEAP components

GlobeNewswire
GlobeNewswire · July 21, 2026 · read →

HAECO signed an Elite Licensed Repair Service Facility agreement with Woodward, authorizing full MRO support for Woodward's fuel control, engine actuation and air-management components across the LEAP-1A, -1B and -1C family. The deal leans on HAECO's Hong Kong and Xiamen footprint to put OEM-approved LEAP component repair closer to the fastest-growing share of the global fleet.
