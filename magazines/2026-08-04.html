AVIATION DAILY

Tuesday, August 4, 2026

Pratt & Whitney's story today is capacity, not chemistry — a hot-section upgrade kit closing in on certification, a new Portuguese GTF shop chasing tripled revenue, and a five-year PW127M deal with Emerald Airlines all point toward an OEM racing to turn engineering fixes into open shop slots. Around it, the LEAP ecosystem keeps decentralizing, with Honeywell and Woodward licensing component repairs out to GMR in India and HAECO in Hong Kong rather than keeping the work in-house. And while a Rolls-Royce business-jet AD tightens life limits the old-fashioned way, the U.S. Air Force's blunt warning about its own engine industrial base is a reminder that durability problems aren't only a commercial-aviation story.

01
FLAGGED FOR YOU

Pratt & Whitney's GTF hot-section upgrade kit tracks to early-2027 certification

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · July 16, 2026 · read →

The roughly 35-part hot-section kit, pulled from the GTF Advantage engine, is on pace for regulatory sign-off by late 2026 or early 2027, with production already underway ahead of certification. Pratt & Whitney says the upgrade is designed to nearly double on-wing life versus the pre-Advantage standard, and initial kits will go first to operators flying in harsh, high-particulate environments.

GTF recovery now hinges on shop capacity, not engineering

Airways Magazine
Airways Magazine · July 15, 2026 · read →

With durability fixes largely defined, Pratt & Whitney's bottleneck has shifted to getting engines through a shop slot — the company says a new additive-manufacturing repair process has cut cycle time over 60%, and it has widened its MRO network with MTU Aero Engines and Delta TechOps. A220 and E2 fleets are recovering quickly, but the article notes the larger A320neo GTF fleet could keep facing disruption for another two to three years.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

OGMA's new GTF engine line in Portugal aims to triple MRO revenue

Aviation Week
Aviation Week · 2026 · read →

Embraer-owned OGMA has stood up a dedicated Pratt & Whitney GTF maintenance line at its Alverca site, the product of more than €90 million invested since 2021 to become an Authorized Maintenance Center for the PW1100G-JM family. OGMA expects the shop to triple its revenue once it reaches full capacity — one more piece of throughput aimed at the GTF shop-visit backlog.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

Pratt & Whitney Canada signs five-year PW127M deal with Emerald Airlines

RTX
RTX / Pratt & Whitney · July 21, 2026 · read →

Announced at Farnborough, the nearly five-year contract covers maintenance, repair and overhaul of PW127M engines powering Emerald Airlines' ATR 72-600 turboprop fleet, with work centered at Pratt & Whitney Canada's Singapore facility and backup support from Montreal. Emerald operates the exclusive regional network for Aer Lingus, so the deal is a direct bet on dispatch reliability for that turboprop feeder operation through 2030.

05

FAA proposes tighter life limits on Rolls-Royce BR700-725 business jet engines

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · proposed rule, July 15, 2026 · read →

Mirroring a 2024 EASA directive, the proposed AD would adopt Rolls-Royce Deutschland's revised airworthiness limitations for the BR700-725A1-12 — the engine behind the Gulfstream G650 — introducing new, more restrictive thresholds and intervals for life-limited parts. The FAA warns that leaving the old limits in place could lead to failure of one or more engines, loss of thrust control, and loss of the airplane; comments are open through August 31.

06

GMR Aero Technic licenses Honeywell LEAP engine control MRO for the Indian market

MRO Business Today
MRO Business Today · late July 2026 · read →

GMR Aero Technic signed a license agreement with Honeywell Aerospace to overhaul seven line-replaceable units used in LEAP engine control systems on A320neo and 737 MAX aircraft, covering full disassembly, inspection, repair, reassembly and functional testing. The deal keeps more LEAP component work in-region for Indian and neighboring carriers, cutting reliance on sending parts overseas for turnaround.

07

HAECO joins Woodward's elite repair network for LEAP engine components

GlobeNewswire
GlobeNewswire · July 21, 2026 · read →

HAECO signed an Elite Licensed Repair Service Facility agreement with Woodward, authorizing OEM-approved repair of fuel control, actuation and air-management components across the LEAP-1A/1B/1C family from its Hong Kong and Xiamen shops. Elite status requires evaluation on technical expertise and track record, adding regional turnaround capacity as the LEAP fleet ages into heavier maintenance demand.

08

SR Technics signs two-year engine maintenance deal with Oman Air

AviTrader
AviTrader · July 22, 2026 · read →

The Swiss MRO provider will handle Oman Air's engine maintenance out of its Zurich facility under a two-year agreement, building on a line-maintenance relationship the two companies have held since 2011. Neither party disclosed the specific engine type covered, but the deal deepens SR Technics' footprint in the Middle East and North Africa region.

09

Korean Air to build $120M widebody-capable MRO hangar at Incheon

Aviation Week
Aviation Week Network · 2026 · read →

Korean Air will invest roughly KRW176 billion (about $120 million) in a new heavy-maintenance hangar at Incheon sized to handle two narrowbodies and one widebody at once, with construction starting in 2027 for a 2029 opening. The facility is one piece of a broader capacity build-out, alongside a separate engine-MRO project, as the carrier absorbs Asiana's fleet.

10

US Air Force warns its fighter-engine industrial base is "significantly challenged"

Defense News
Defense News · August 3, 2026 · read →

A new Air Force Request for Information cites "production delays, quality control issues, and critical obsolescence across key engine components," pointing to F-35 engine shortfalls and $3 billion in B-52 re-engining cost growth as evidence. The Air Force wants to buy 180-plus engines a year by 2034 and is soliciting a multiyear, competitive sourcing strategy — comments are due August 28.
