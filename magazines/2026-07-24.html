AVIATION DAILY

Friday, July 24, 2026

Farnborough's books close at $84.7 billion and 904 firm engine orders, but the durability story keeps writing itself in the fine print — a fresh AD on Pratt & Whitney Canada's PW210 turbine exhaust frame and a proposed directive rewriting life limits on Rolls-Royce's BR700 fan disc, both landing quietly beside the show-floor totals. Underneath that paperwork the MRO network is visibly retooling: SR Technics adds Oman Air to its Zurich engine shop, StandardAero locks in Avolon's LEAP fleet, and HAECO joins Woodward's elite repair tier, even as Pratt & Whitney previews ceramic matrix composite blades for the next GTF. Order totals make the headlines, but shop capacity and life-limited parts are still what decide the program economics.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA orders repetitive inspections of P&W Canada PW210 turbine exhaust frames

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 14, 2026 · read →

The AD covers PW210A, PW210A1 and PW210S engines after operators reported circumferential cracks on the turbine exhaust frame's external surface, traced to thermal stress from repeated engine starts. It requires repetitive visual inspections, with any crack finding triggering frame replacement — the engines power Sikorsky S-76D and Leonardo AW169 helicopters, and Canada issued an emergency order on the same defect in June.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA proposes new life limits for Rolls-Royce BR700-715 fan disc after uncontained-failure risk

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · comments due August 20, 2026 · read →

The proposed rule would supersede a 2009 directive for BR700-715A1-30, -715B1-30 and -715C1-30 engines — which power the Gulfstream G550 — after Rolls-Royce revised its time-limits manual with new, more restrictive retirement thresholds for the low-pressure compressor (fan) disc assembly. Left unaddressed, the FAA warns of an uncontained disc failure and resulting airplane damage.

Pratt & Whitney to bring ceramic matrix composites and composite blades to the next GTF

Leeham News
Leeham News and Analysis · July 20, 2026 · read →

P&W's NextGen GTF program will adopt CMC hot-section components and composite fan/compressor blades, technology GE pioneered on the GE9X and GEnx, as it chases another step-change in fuel burn and durability beyond the Advantage upgrade. The shift signals P&W closing a materials gap with GE and Rolls-Royce as all three OEMs race toward their next clean-sheet architectures.

AeroTime · July 21, 2026 · read →

All work under the new agreement will run through SR Technics' engine maintenance facility in Zurich, extending a relationship that began with line maintenance support back in 2011. HAECO joins Woodward's elite repair network for CFM LEAP components

GlobeNewswire
Woodward / HAECO · July 21, 2026 · read →

The Elite Licensed Repair Service Facility agreement authorizes HAECO to service Woodward's full fuel control, engine actuation and air management portfolio across the LEAP-1A, -1B and -1C family. Leveraging its Hong Kong and Xiamen sites, HAECO is positioning to catch component-repair demand from the fast-growing Asia-Pacific LEAP fleet.

AviTrader · July 22, 2026 · read →

The deal covers Avolon's roughly 150 delivered A320neo (LEAP-1A) and 54 737 MAX (LEAP-1B) aircraft, giving the lessor's customers priority induction booking and not-to-exceed pricing on defined shop-visit workscopes. StandardAero, a CFM LEAP Premier MRO provider, has already completed multiple LEAP-1A/-1B PRSV events under its 2023 CFM Branded Service Agreement.

Aviation Business News · July 21, 2026 · read →

PAL signed an MOU for 18 Trent XWB-97 engines to power nine firm A350-1000s, with the deal extendable to five more aircraft under purchase rights, plus Rolls-Royce's TotalCare health-monitoring and maintenance package. Rolls-Royce
Rolls-Royce · July 20, 2026 · read →

Rolls-Royce, Avio Aero and IHI completed a key design-review round for the sixth-generation fighter's power and propulsion system, finalizing the centreline engine demonstrator design after more than a hundred subscale component tests.
