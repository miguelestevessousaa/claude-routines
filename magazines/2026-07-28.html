A decade into fixing durability issues on the PW1100G, Pratt & Whitney continues investing hundreds of millions of dollars building, expanding, and licensing MRO facilities to resolve component wear that has hit the A320neo family hardest, with related issues on the A220 and E195-E2. FAA orders repetitive inspections of Pratt & Whitney Canada PW210 engines for exhaust frame cracking

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 14, 2026 · read →

The directive covers all PW210A, PW210A1, and PW210S engines after circumferential cracks turned up at the turbine exhaust frame's external surface, traced to thermal stress from engine starts. Operators must carry out repetitive visual inspections and replace any frame showing cracking to prevent frame failure.

FLAGGED FOR YOU

FAA mandates fixes on Rolls-Royce Trent XWB fuel manifold after in-flight engine fire

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · effective July 17, 2026 · read →

The AD covers Trent XWB-75, -79, -79B, -84 and -97 engines after a main fuel hose assembly in the fuel manifold degraded in service, causing a controlled in-flight fire, heat damage to the nacelle's thrust-reverser C-ducts, and a commanded engine shutdown. The -97 powers the A350-1000 and the -84 the A350-900, putting a meaningful share of the in-service widebody fleet under the inspection mandate.

Federal Register
Federal Aviation Administration · published July 17, 2026 · read →

The FAA's directive follows a reported fuel leak traced to a defective fuel tube hose and requires operators to identify and replace every affected hose from service. GlobeNewswire
HAECO / Woodward / GlobeNewswire · July 21, 2026 · read →
